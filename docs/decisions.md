## Why 7 was reversed

iroh needs relay servers that someone runs. That conflicts with hosting on
Cloudflare only, and the plan assumed VMs without raising it. Browsers
cannot make direct iroh connections, and calls need WebRTC anyway, so iroh
meant a second networking stack.

## Why 9 was reversed

Three UI codebases, each carrying three themes, was the largest cost in the
plan. A native plugin per platform keeps full Core Haptics and Android
haptics under a single shared UI, and the web view brings WebRTC for calls
without a C++ dependency.

## Why 14 was reversed (15)

Efficiency is what keeps hosting cheap at scale. Cloudflare bills per
request ($0.15 per million to a Durable Object, $0.30 per million to a Worker),
per database row written ($1.00 per million; reads are 1,000 times cheaper)
and per stored GB ($0.20 a month). Every row and request saved per message
is money saved at volume. The discipline applies on devices too, where it
buys battery and responsiveness.

## Why device-to-device messaging was dropped (8)

Done on 2026-11-06: iroh is gone, and with it 156 of 540 dependencies.

With WebSocket push from Cloudflare's edge, a message crosses roughly the
same internet path as a direct connection would. The costs of a direct path
outweigh the few milliseconds it saves:

- every contact learns your IP address
- connections must be kept alive, which drains batteries
- address exchange is extra machinery

Direct paths remain where they matter: calls and large files.
